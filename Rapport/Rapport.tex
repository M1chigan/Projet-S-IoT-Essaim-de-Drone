\section*{Objective}

To determine the molar enthalpy of neutralisation, $\Delta H_\text{neut}$, for
the reaction between hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide, and to compare
the measured value against the accepted literature value for a strong
acid--strong base neutralisation~\cite{atkins2018}.

\section*{Method}

\SI{50}{\milli\liter} of \SI{1.0}{\mole\per\liter} \ce{HCl} and
\SI{50}{\milli\liter} of \SI{1.0}{\mole\per\liter} \ce{NaOH} were each
brought to room temperature and their initial temperatures recorded
separately. The \ce{NaOH} solution was added rapidly to the \ce{HCl} in an
insulated calorimeter, stirred continuously, and the temperature was
recorded every 15 seconds for 5 minutes.

The overall reaction is:
\begin{equation}
  \ce{HCl (aq) + NaOH (aq) -> NaCl (aq) + H2O (l)}
\end{equation}

\section*{Data}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Temperature readings during neutralisation}
  \begin{tabular}{S[table-format=2.0] S[table-format=2.1]}
    \toprule
    {Time (s)} & {Temperature (\si{\celsius})} \\
    \midrule
    0   & 21.4 \\
    15  & 24.8 \\
    30  & 27.1 \\
    45  & 27.9 \\
    60  & 28.0 \\
    90  & 27.8 \\
    120 & 27.5 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The maximum temperature (extrapolated back to the moment of mixing to
correct for heat loss) was taken as \SI{28.3}{\celsius}, giving a temperature
change of $\Delta T = \SI{6.9}{\celsius}$.

\section*{Calculations}

Assuming the combined solution has the density and specific heat capacity
of water ($\rho = \SI{1.00}{\gram\per\milli\liter}$,
$c = \SI{4.18}{\joule\per\gram\per\kelvin}$), the heat released is:

\begin{equation}
  q = mc\Delta T = (100~\text{g})(\SI{4.18}{\joule\per\gram\per\kelvin})(6.9~\text{K}) = \SI{2884}{\joule}
\end{equation}

Moles of water formed (limiting reagent, either acid or base, both
\SI{0.050}{\mole}):

\begin{equation}
  n = \SI{0.050}{\liter} \times \SI{1.0}{\mole\per\liter} = \SI{0.050}{\mole}
\end{equation}

Molar enthalpy of neutralisation:

\begin{equation}
  \Delta H_\text{neut} = -\frac{q}{n} = -\frac{\SI{2884}{\joule}}{\SI{0.050}{\mole}} = \SI{-57.7}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}
\end{equation}

\section*{Results and Discussion}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Comparison with the literature value}
  \begin{tabular}{lS[table-format=-2.1]}
    \toprule
    {Source} & {$\Delta H_\text{neut}$ (\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole})} \\
    \midrule
    This experiment      & -57.7 \\
    Literature~\cite{atkins2018} & -57.3 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The measured value of \SI{-57.7}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} agrees with the
accepted literature value of \SI{-57.3}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} to within
0.7\%. The small discrepancy is most likely due to heat loss to the
calorimeter and surroundings despite insulation, and to the extrapolation
used to estimate the maximum temperature, since the reaction rate at these
concentrations is fast enough that some heat is lost before the true peak
temperature is reached.

\section*{Conclusion}

The molar enthalpy of neutralisation of \ce{HCl} and \ce{NaOH} was measured
as \SI{-57.7}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, consistent with the expected value for
a strong acid--strong base reaction, confirming that the reaction is
exothermic and that the result is independent of the specific acid and base
used, as both dissociate completely in solution.
