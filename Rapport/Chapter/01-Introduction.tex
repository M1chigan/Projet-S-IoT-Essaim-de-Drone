\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}

When technological advancements on drones are growing exponentially particularly for defence usages, the study of drone swarms is more relevant than ever. Their applications are numerous : it goes from monitoring to artistic performances and rescuing. An interesting ability of drone swarms can be to carry heavy loads by coordinating the movement of multiple drones. Our project therefore lies within that application of utilizing drone swarms for carrying loads. It aims to implement drone swarms techniques based on Ultrawide Band technology (UWB) and Xbee modules. \\ \noindent The first objective of the project is to get familiar with the hardware available and then work out algorithms to make multiple drones fly collectively. Two main questions emerge from reflecting on the implementation of a drone swarm. Firstly, to behave collectively, each drone has to be aware of its positioning relatively to the others (or at least the neighbouring drones). Moreover, it is necessary for them to communicate with each other. Therefore, a technological choice has to be made to fulfil these two requirements. To do so, we have been imposed to work with two technologies (UWB and Xbee) and therefore, we will study how they can be helpful for responding to these problematic points. 
\\ \noindent This midterm report will cover all the research and work done since the beginning of the project in September. We will first present the hardware used as well as the specifications of the project. The bibliographical study will then be exposed followed by the current state of the work done, covering UWB, measure processing, inter-module communication, data visualisation, simulations and future tests.
